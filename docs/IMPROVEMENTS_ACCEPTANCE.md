# Improvement acceptance and human review

Prepared 28 September 2026. This is an **uncompleted review protocol**, not evidence of clinical benefit, usability success, native-language approval or a clinical release. Automated results live in PROGRESS.md and the evaluation reports. Record a reviewer, date, app revision and evidence for every completed row below.

## What the existing evidence establishes

The vocabulary has 124 distinct complete-message meanings across ten categories, with English, Tamil and Tanglish retrieval aliases. All Tamil content remains a draft pending native review. The controlled regression contains 120 authored input scenarios evaluated in both output languages: 240 executions passed in the recorded development run. This is not a held-out set, 240 independent real-world observations, an STT test or proof of arbitrary input accuracy. See [fixture report](../evals/intent/report.md).

The installed `llama3:latest` selector benchmark completed zero of 20 eligible model requests within the eight-second model budget. All 20 used the explicitly labelled catalog fallback; four additional cases clarified without a model call. Returned meanings stayed within the controlled set. No model ranking accuracy or Tamil generation quality was measured. See [local selector report](../evals/intent/report-local-selector.md).

The frozen source now retains up to three distinct complete recognition alternatives, including the primary, when they fit the 120-character context limit. Missing segment alternatives use that segment's best transcript; the continued-listening prefix is retained. Overlong alternatives are omitted, while raw input is bounded to 500 characters. This preserves recognizer wording; it does not establish recognition accuracy. Personal words now support aliases, a use/location description, search across those fields, and reversible hiding from the patient picker. Edits return content to approval. Partner understanding and the partner's next question are separate inputs, and the recorded communication outcome can retain the optional understanding note.

Recorded engineering verification at source freeze: 358 unit tests (348 in the complete run plus ten focused transcript tests), 26 browser checks (24 in the complete suite plus two targeted review regressions), workspace lint, all three workspace typechecks and production build passed. The compiled PWA also passed browser-offline checks for Home, a first visit to Words, a negative typed request and None-of-these exclusion. These are software/browser results on the development machine, not human, phone, speech-quality or clinical acceptance. See PROGRESS.md for the final evidence record.

## Twelve review checks

All twelve human checks are **pending**. Software features and synthetic tests enable these checks; they do not replace them.

| # | Review task | What to observe and record | Acceptance or escalation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Native Tamil and bilingual meaning review | A Tamil-speaking reviewer checks every displayed/spoken catalog sentence, aliases, register, name pronunciation, polarity, body part/side and English gloss. An SLP or appropriate clinician reviews sensitive health wording. Use LANGUAGE_REVIEW.md, including new UI strings. | Each reviewed entry gets a decision, reviewer and date. Resolve meaning changes before a patient trial. A machine translation/back-translation is not approval. |
| 2 | Respectful choice and supported approval | Present a new personal word, story and passport item as drafts; support the person's own approval, refusal and changed mind. Try an unfamiliar symbol. | Meaning and approval are confirmed in an accessible way; caregiver entry alone does not count as patient endorsement. Record unclear symbols and preferred text/photo alternatives. |
| 3 | Exact-message playback control | Select a sentence, change one word/language, open a photo, restore a draft and receive a partner question. Try Stop and a fresh final tap. | No background or stale-authorized patient speech. Visible text, actual audio and language agree. Any unsafe playback blocks trial use until repaired. |
| 4 | Shared meaning beyond speech output | Person chooses an intended message privately or with an independent facilitator; partner explains what they understood before hearing the intended answer. Person confirms, corrects, declines or remains unsure. | Record intended meaning, independent partner interpretation and the person's endorsement separately. Delivery, audio start and a partner's confidence are not shared understanding. |
| 5 | Refusal, uncertainty and repair | Try “no water”, “not now”, “I don't know”, a wrong-person correction and None of these. Then deliberately reconsider an earlier choice using Undo/Back. | No polarity reversal or rejected paraphrase returns unexpectedly. Repair preserves unchanged parts where supported; otherwise the UI clearly asks for a complete correction. Choosing not to answer is respected, not scored as a communication failure. |
| 6 | Personal relevance and retrieval | Let the person choose familiar people, places, foods, interests and one social/story message. Find entries by Tamil, Tanglish, category, photo, first letters and a personal use/location description where relevant. Hide and restore one personal word. | Record useful coverage, missing concepts and false matches. No invented person or event; aliases retrieve the same meaning without erasing a meaningful distinction. Hiding preserves the entry without showing it in the patient picker. Quantity of words is not the outcome. |
| 7 | Physical and visual access | On the actual phone/tablet, try each enabled patient route with preferred hand, larger text, chosen choice count, repeat-tap setting, keyboard/switch access and relevant assistive technology. | Readable text and focus are not covered by fixed controls; stable Help/Stop remain reachable; patient targets meet project sizes. Record accidental selections and assistance. Automated axe passes alone do not establish accessibility. |
| 8 | Pacing, fatigue and continuity | Let the person pause, finish speech input, continue it, switch to typing/drawing and resume after navigation or refresh. Check that complete recognition alternatives retain negation and the continued prefix. Offer a break without asking them to finish a task. | Preserve the draft without autoplay. Record reported effort and requested breaks; never infer fatigue, cognition or severity from time taken. If a route cannot restore its draft, document the limit before use. Recognizer alternatives are uncertain transcripts, not confirmed meanings. |
| 9 | Photo, story and conversation support | Select a photo hotspot through both image and list, show a story one line at a time, display/print a passport and retain one partner question. | Author-approved text stays exact; images do not become inferred facts; labels and hotspots are comprehensible. Partner prompts remain separate from the person's answer. |
| 10 | Offline and unavailable-provider use | Prepare the production app, disable the network, reopen enabled tools, find saved words/photos, show text, and try a checked exact recording and an installed local voice. Also test a missing voice. | Record actual working assets on the target device. No silent substitute voice or sample transcript; missing speech leaves text usable. A cached index page or `navigator.onLine` alone is not readiness. |
| 11 | Personal data, backup and caregiver delivery | Edit/delete an approved item, inspect encrypted export/import, try a conflicting import, cancel a queued Help, reconnect and reload the caregiver view. Use fictional data first. | Edits invalidate approval as designed; import is reviewable and does not overwrite silently. No stale Help alarm or duplicate delivery. Received, spoken, alert tone and understood states stay distinct. Check what remains in logs/exports. |
| 12 | Fair comparison and participant preference | Compare the same intended messages against the person's usual method or a suitable personalized board, with matched Tamil/English content, familiarity, access setup and partner help. Ask which method they prefer and why. | Report individual outcomes and unresolved problems, including failures/stops. A faster audio start is not evidence of better communication or recovery. Do not claim superiority from an unmatched baseline. |

## Exploratory sessions with six to eight person/partner pairs

This is a formative design proposal, not a powered clinical trial or a prescribed sample size. Begin only after language/content review and appropriate local arrangements for supported consent. Include the person with aphasia in consent and design decisions; a diagnosis or slow response does not establish impaired decision-making capacity. Offer accessible explanations, a familiar support person where wanted, a clear stop choice and separate agreement for recording. Seek the appropriate research/ethics advice if the work becomes research; this document does not determine legal or institutional requirements.

Recruit for useful variation in preferred language, literacy, motor/visual access and communication methods, without claiming the small group represents aphasia generally. Record each person's preferred Tamil register and any English/Tanglish use. Use participant-selected, non-sensitive tasks plus a few agreed contrasts: request/refusal, uncertainty, repair, social connection and a personal story. Avoid invented medical scenarios unless the person and clinician find them appropriate.

Give matched familiarization and practice with both methods. For six pairs assign three Sollu-first and three comparison-first; for eight pairs assign four each. Counterbalance equivalent task sets as well as method order. Use the same partner and level of available help, while recording prompts that actually occur. Match vocabulary and intended meanings rather than imposing English word order on Tamil. The built-in baseline is a selectable word board, not a validated or universally appropriate comparator.

Offer short blocks and flexible breaks. Do not require a fixed number of completed tasks. Stop immediately on the person's request, discomfort, repeated unintended audio or a serious meaning error; preserve the error for engineering review only with agreed data handling. Never ask participants to continue so that a target denominator can be reached.

## Outcome record

Use participant codes, not names, and minimize retained content. Raw audio/video is optional and requires separate agreement; no participant recordings belong in source control. These are custom exploratory observations, not validated clinical scores.

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Session | App revision, device/browser, participant code, method/order, task-set ID, preferred language, access settings, partner/facilitator roles |
| Meaning | Participant/facilitator's agreed intended meaning; partner's independent interpretation; person-endorsed match / partial / mismatch / unsure / declined |
| Repair and agency | Wrong selections, rejected candidates, repair steps, final correction, refusal/changed mind and whether each was respected |
| Assistance | Independent, requested help or unsolicited prompting; type of help and who provided it; keep patient and partner judgments separate |
| Effort and preference | Person's own accessible report and optional partner report, requested breaks, preferred method and reason; do not infer ratings from speed |
| Secondary measures | Time to endorsed shared understanding, taps, audio-start latency, route used, offline/provider state, stopped/incomplete reason |
| Follow-up | Severity of product problem, reproduction notes, proposed correction, reviewer and resolution/retest date |

Report the number of offered/started, completed, stopped, declined and failed attempts separately. Keep failed/stopped attempts visible; state the denominator for every rate. Summarize each pair before any pooled description. A refusal task succeeds when refusal is accurately conveyed; declining participation remains separate from task success or failure. With six to eight exploratory pairs, avoid significance, population-level or clinical-benefit claims. A later prospectively specified study is needed for such questions.

## Evidence boundaries and remaining acceptance work

- [ASHA AAC](https://www.asha.org/Practice-Portal/Professional-Issues/Augmentative-and-Alternative-Communication/) supports individualized functional vocabulary and access assessment; it does not prescribe this catalog size or guarantee these widgets help.
- [Aphasia Institute supported conversation](https://www.aphasia.ca/communication-tools-communicative-access-sca/) and the [primary SCA training trial](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/1092-4388%282001/051%29) concern supported communication/partner training. Reading in-app hints is not certified training or evidence that Sollu reproduces those outcomes.
- [ROMA-2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36583427/) concerns a core communication outcome for aphasia research. This custom task protocol is not the Scenario Test, and no Tamil adaptation is claimed validated.
- [Big CACTUS](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1474442219301929) separates practiced word retrieval gains from conversation outcomes. Sollu's expanded vocabulary is assistive content, not evidence of word-learning therapy or recovery.

Pending beyond software verification: native/SLP sign-off, consented speech-recognition clips, actual Tamil voice listening, real-device offline/assistive-technology checks, supported sessions, participant-driven revisions and an independent held-out semantic corpus. No human sessions, clinical instruments or benefit measurements have been completed by this build.
